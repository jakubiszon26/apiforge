## Goal

Run results and Problems can be dragged up to the top of their workspace
and dropped there to take it, the way KTLO-19's and KTLO-18's buttons do,
and dragged back down to return. The edge the owner reached for after
KTLO-18 and KTLO-19 now does what the button does.

## What the owner will see

- New, Run results (Code, Diagram and Split): dragging the panel's top edge
  or its grip past the normal ceiling keeps following the pointer. Within
  64 px of the workspace's top the strip left above the panel shows the drop
  zone *Release to fill the workspace* (soft accent fill, accent outline,
  bold accent text) and the grip is outlined in accent. Releasing there
  maximises the panel exactly as *Maximise run results* does: the button
  reads *Restore run results*, the board and code are not drawn, and
  Escape, the button, another tab or another mode restore it. Maximised, the
  grip stays at the panel's top; dragging it down restores the panel and
  continues the drag, so the panel lands where it is released, within its
  normal range.
- New, Problems (Code and Split): dragging its edge past its normal range
  (360 px) continues up to KTLO-18's ceiling, the code panel's height minus
  the strip of code. Within 64 px of that ceiling the strip shows the drop
  zone *Release to expand problems*; releasing there expands the section as
  *Expand problems* does, the button reads *Restore problems*, and the strip
  of code stays above it (decided). Dragging the edge down from the expanded
  state works as today.
- Changed: a panel released above its normal ceiling but outside the zone
  returns to the ceiling (Run results: 560 px or the window less 200 px;
  Problems: 360 px), as a drag past the ceiling does today.
- Removed or hidden: nothing. Migrated or converted: nothing. Default
  behaviour that changes: none; the remembered normal heights are kept and
  nothing new is remembered.

## Technical appendix

- `crates/apiforge-desktop/src/report.rs::show`: take `workspace =
  ui.available_rect_before_wrap()` before the panel; a `drag:
  Option<Drag { height_before: f32, snapped: bool }>` on `ReportPanel`.
  While the grip (`handle.dragged()`) or egui's own edge
  (`ui.ctx().read_response(panel_id.with("__resize"))`, the pattern
  `code_view.rs` already uses) is dragged, the panel's `size_range` top is
  lifted to `full - 8.0` for that frame and the height follows the drag
  delta; `snapped = panel_top <= workspace.top() + DROP_SNAP` with
  `DROP_SNAP: f32 = 64.0`. On `drag_stopped()`: snapped → `maximised = true`
  and the height returns to `height_before`; otherwise the height is clamped
  to `maximum` and `size_epoch` bumps, so egui forgets the lifted size.
- The zone: `widgets::drop_zone(ui, rect, label)` paints the mock-up's
  frame (`theme::ACCENT_SOFT` fill, 1.5 px `theme::ACCENT` stroke, 6 px
  corners, bold `theme::FONT_BODY` text in the accent) on the foreground
  layer and allocates the rect as a non-interactive labelled widget
  (`WidgetType::Label`, `Sense::hover()`), so tests find it by label and it
  takes no click. The grip's accent outline is the one it already paints
  when focused.
- Maximised: the grip branch (`was_open && !maximised`) is extended to the
  maximised panel; a drag with `delta.y > 0` sets `maximised = false`,
  `height = full - delta.y` and bumps the epoch; the grip's id
  (`ui.id().with("report-resize-handle")`) is the same in both states, so
  the drag continues on the new panel.
- `folder/code_view.rs::code_area`: while the resize response of
  `diagnostics` is dragged, the range becomes `start..=ceiling` (today
  `PROBLEMS_HEIGHTS` or the expanded range); `snapped = height >= ceiling -
  DROP_SNAP`; the zone is drawn over the code strip's rect (the central
  panel's top `code_strip(ui)` pixels); on release, snapped →
  `problems_expanded = true; problems_dragged = false; problems_epoch += 1`
  (the button then reads *Restore problems* and the strip stays), otherwise
  the height clamps to `PROBLEMS_HEIGHTS.end()` and the epoch bumps.
  `workbench.panels.diagnostics` is written only when not expanded, as
  today, so the lifted height is never remembered.
- `restore()` keeps returning to the normal height; `run_report`'s rule
  (another tab or mode restores) and `restore_report_on_escape` are
  unchanged. The mock-up's `mock_ux00::Drag` switch and `MOCK_DRAG_GAP` are
  not merged; the review view `review_ux05_drag_to_workspace` is rewritten
  with a real pointer drag and added to `testing::review`.

## Implementation notes

Choices made while building it, each within the decisions above; the QA
report lists them as deviations from the appendix.

- **The drag leaves at least 32 px above Run results** (`DROP_ZONE_MIN`),
  not 8 px: the zone is drawn in the strip above the panel, and 8 px would
  not hold its words. The panel follows the pointer up to 32 px under the
  workspace's top; the snap distance is 64 px as decided.
- **The grip follows the pointer by position**, as egui's edge does, no
  longer by each frame's movement: a pointer taken past the end of the range
  (above the 32 px, or below the least height) found the panel offset from
  it when it came back. The panel's top stays where the press landed on the
  grip.
- **A release that maximises gives up the grip's keyboard focus**, so the
  released panel shows its grip as the mock-up does (grey, no outline).
  A click on the grip still focuses it, as before.
- **Nothing is drawn above Run results while a drag holds them over their
  ceiling**: the board's zoom bar, the library and the panels' edges
  overflowed the short strip onto the panel's bar. The strip shows the
  workspace's background and, near the top, the zone, as the approved
  mid-drag frame does; a release outside the zone draws the views again.
- **The height to come back to is no longer taken from the panel every
  frame**, only while its edge is dragged (the grip, the keys and a release
  set it themselves), and a release outside the zone keeps to the highest
  ceiling the drag saw. A notice under the tab strip during storage work
  (*Checking external changes…*) shortened the workspace for a moment and,
  taken every frame, shortened the remembered height for good; CI showed it.
  For the same reason egui's own memory of the panel's size is dropped
  unless its edge is being dragged: egui prefers the size a panel was last
  drawn at to the one asked for, so a frame drawn short kept it short. A
  side effect: at 960 × 540 the keys and the grip now reach the least
  height of 120 px; egui had held the panel at the 185 px its contents
  first took.
- **A drag that ends without a release** (the panel goes away under it)
  maximises and expands nothing.
- **Problems with no rows** keep only the height of their caption and hint
  after any release outside the zone, as they do today (egui sizes a bottom
  panel to its contents when they are shorter); criterion 3's 360 px case
  is tested with a document that has problems. Expanding by the drag works
  either way.
- Tests: the drag helper rests the pointer one frame after the last move,
  because the grip's height is drawn on the frame after it is taken.
  Criterion 9 focuses the grip before each key, because an arrow key also
  moves egui's focus on (today's behaviour, unchanged). Criterion 7 adds
  a release 500 px down at 1600 × 1000, under the ceiling.
