## What worked

- **Measuring before and after.** LAY-01's command gave every layout ticket
  a number. It ended two tickets before they cost a review: LAY-04 showed
  that routing changes barely move real boards, and LAY-05 was dropped on
  that measurement.
- **The mid-sprint review and the checkpoint.** Reading the plan again on
  the real board after five days replaced the waves with "a coherent board
  first"; after the checkpoint the owner's notes from real use became
  LAY-29 to LAY-34 before part B went on.
- **Chips instead of wires for a shared value** (LAY-23, LAY-25), a ticket
  that was not in the plan: `shop-30` drawn with every wire has 42 wires
  and 76 crossings, with chips 9 and 0.
- **Shortening instead of drifting.** When part B kept growing, the owner
  cut the sprint to a minimal set with one rule — no new geometry — and the
  set merged in a day.
- **Quizzes on captures.** Decisions put as questions with a recommended
  option were, as in KTLO, answered within the day.

## What did not

- **The acceptance ran on a shape no reference board had.** Every board the
  sprint measured has a dense data flow. The owner's scenario has none:
  sixteen independent requests. On it "folded by default" leaves rows 540
  to 775 px apart and a Fit of 36 %, and what matters is not on the board
  at all. Four board defects and a whole sprint of findings appeared the
  day the sprint closed. — allowed by: nothing in planning asks which
  shapes of scenario an acceptance covers, or asks the owner for the shape
  of their own work before the references are chosen.
- **The sprint was shortened and its acceptance replaced twice**, and the
  exit criteria in the backlog stayed unticked through both. The last
  criterion was never met as written and the record did not say so until
  now. — allowed by: no rule for how a replaced acceptance is recorded;
  "a ticked criterion names its evidence" says nothing about a criterion
  that is replaced.
- **Option A of LAY-29 was decided and withdrawn a day later, after it was
  built.** Its costs (4 → 6 crossings, Fit 51 → 49 %) were measured on
  fixtures that LANG-13 rewrites, which is why it was withdrawn; the half
  that was dropped — an ordering line starting on a card's top edge — had
  no issue of its own, and the owner met it in the run (LAY-45). — allowed
  by: a decision's measurement does not say whether its fixtures are about
  to change; a withdrawn decision or a narrowed ticket does not have to
  file what it drops.
- **"Folded by default" was decided without measuring the board it
  leaves.** The default was reversed on 2026-11-16 and restored on
  2026-11-18; neither question showed the tidied board in the folded
  state, where Tidy up still kept every folded check's room (LAY-44). —
  allowed by: the stop condition asks the owner about a changed default
  but not for a capture and a metric row of the state the default
  produces.
- **First-round drawings stood in for design-review captures.** The fifteen
  decisions of 2026-11-19 were taken on drawings, and for DIAG-02, REP-07
  and REP-08 the drawing is the approval. Planning allows a drawing only
  for a screen that does not exist yet and only before a capture; these
  three change views that exist. The drawing of LAY-44 states a Fit of
  65–70 % that is an estimate, against a criterion of 60 %. — allowed by:
  the order of preference in *Mock-ups* is not tied to what a mock-up may
  approve.
- **The planning model proposed nothing of its own until asked.** It placed
  every finding of the run in a ticket and did not say that a table of
  cases would remove most of the scenario; the owner named it. The
  proposals that followed (LANG-17 to LANG-19, REQ-07 to REQ-10, GEN-01)
  were all accepted into scope within a day. — allowed by: no rule that an
  analysis carries proposals beyond what was raised.
- **Scope more than doubled.** 29 issues joined 21. Each was decided and
  recorded, and the capacity line was amended, but the 60-minute limit on
  work awaiting acceptance was lifted by the owner for 2026-11-16 and the
  sprint's review minutes were never added up at the end.
- **Two ignored tests wait for a decision on a closed ticket.**
  `lay39_criterion_5` and `lay39_criterion_6` say "owner decision pending"
  and name LAY-39, whose issue #435 is closed.

## What surprised us

- A validation test — the most ordinary kind of API test — was absent from
  two weeks of work on reading scenarios.
- `fix` to `feat` at 0.86 with 149 `test` commits: the acceptance tests
  were written first and still did not describe what the owner then found,
  because they described the reference boards.
- The geometry that took the largest pull requests (LAY-03 at 4 126 lines,
  LAY-06, LAY-31) is the part to be measured again after LANG-13
  ([lay.md 13.6](../sprints/lay.md#136-to-measure-again-after-lang-13)):
  34 of the 52 ordering edges of the reference scenarios leave with
  `@after`.
- Twelve pull requests above 2 000 lines passed as `S` or `M`. Lines are
  not a limit since KTLO-28, but no ticket recorded what the review
  actually cost.
